A Small-Apartment Setup

Where to put a fountain and feeder in 45 square meters, from power cables to mats, so a small home stays calm and clean.

In 45 square meters, every surface is spoken for, so a fountain and feeder should earn their place. The best spot for the fountain is the kitchen or the edge of the living space, somewhere you pass often but do not trip over. It needs to be near an outlet, away from direct sun, and on a hard floor or a mat rather than carpet. Keep it off the route the cat already uses. A counter works if the cat will jump; otherwise a low, stable surface is better.

Power is the quiet constraint. A fountain and a feeder both need a socket, and in a small apartment the nearest one is rarely in the right place. Route the 1.8 m braided cable along a baseboard or behind a cabinet rather than across open floor, and leave a little slack so a bumped unit does not pull the plug. If the cable must cross a doorway, run it flat and tape it down. A cable that looks tidy to you will mostly go ignored by the cat.

Cats do not want their water next to their food. It is an old instinct: a kill left near a water source spoils it, so a cat in the wild keeps the two apart, and a cat in a studio keeps the habit. Give the fountain and the feeder their own corners, ideally a meter or two apart. The feeder can sit on a low shelf or a corner of the kitchen while the fountain takes the far end of the living space. Separate them, and both get used more.

The noise question answers itself once you hear the fountain. At 24 decibels at one meter, it sits below quiet conversation and above silence, a soft trickle most people stop noticing within days. If your bedroom is close, think about where the sound lands. A fountain against a wall can reflect sound; one on a mat with a little mass under it does not. If you are a light sleeper, put the fountain on the far side of the room, and let the white noise work for you.

Finish with the mat. A Still Mat under the fountain and another under the feeder catches splashes, crumbs, and the small mess of daily use, and keeps both units from creeping across a smooth floor. Put the mat slightly wider than the unit, with the raised lip facing out so spills stay on the mat, not the wood. Then step back and check the plan: water and food in opposite corners, cables along the walls, mats under both, and a clear path between door and window.

Opposite corners, cables along the wall, mats under both — that is the small-apartment plan.
